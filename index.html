Australian Terrestrial Protected Areas

Background

Protected areas are an important part of Australia's biodiversity conservation system and play a key role in maintaining the health of ecosystems, protecting native species and natural landscapes. However, the distribution and management of protected areas are not balanced, and there are significant differences among different regions in terms of protection coverage, governance methods, and ecological protection effectiveness. This dashboard will explore the spatial distribution characteristics of protected areas in Australia, compare the differences among states and territories, and assess whether the current protected area network can fully and reasonably support biodiversity conservation.

1.Distribution Map Of Protected Areas in Australia

Land protected areas in Australia are widely distributed across states and territories, but there are significant differences in their quantity and spatial distribution. The protected areas in Tasmania and Victoria are the densest, with large areas included in the protection scope. Western Australia has the largest protected area in the country, while the protected areas in the Northern Territory are relatively sparsely distributed and have a lower coverage.

Protected Areas By State

2.Number of Protected Areas by Jurisdiction

The number of protected areas under the jurisdiction of Victoria is the highest, while that under the jurisdiction of the Australian Capital Territory is the lowest.

3.Total Protected Area by State

Western Australia has the largest protected area, while the Australian Capital Territory has the smallest protected area.

4.Protected Area vs Total Area by State

Overall, the coverage rate of protected areas in the austrlian capital territory is the highest, accounting for approximately 56% of the total area, while in Queensland it is only about 9%.

Protected Area Coverage and Governance

5.Protected Area Coverage Across Australia IBRA Regions

Note:IBRA (Interim Biogeographic Regionalisation for Australia) divides Australia into ecological regions based on biodiversity and environmental characteristics

The protection coverage rate varies greatly across different regions of IBRA. In some areas, the protection coverage rate exceeds 80%, but in most areas, it is below 20%

6.Protected Area Governance Structure

The governance methods of protected areas in Australia can be classified into four types. 49% of protected areas are governed by communities, while a few are privately or jointly governed.

7.Top 10 IBRA Bioregions by Protected Area Coverage

Note:The International Union for Conservation of Nature classifies protected areas based on its management objectives. Classes Ia and Ib focus on strict protection, while Classes V and VI promote interaction between humans and nature through sustainable utilization and management.

category VI covers the major bioregions in Australia, especially the desert areas, while the more stringent conservation categories(la-lb) only occupy a very small area.

The Connection Between Protected Areas And Animals

8.Threatened Species Status by Animal Group

As can be seen from the chart, Australia has a large number of threatened species, which are distributed among different animal groups. This reflects the richness of Australia's biodiversity and highlights the importance of improving the network of protected areas and protecting key habitats.

9.Ghost Bat Records and Major Protected Areas

Note:Ghost Bat was chosen because it is one of the representative threatened animal in Australia. Its activity range covers most state of Australia, and its survival is highly dependent on specific habitats, such as caves, crevices and surrounding foraging areas. Therefore, using Ghost Bat as a case can more clearly analyze whether the protected area truly covers the key habitats, rather than just looking at the area of the protected area itself.

It can be seen from the figure that most of the habitats of Ghost Bat are distributed within the main protected areas, indicating that the existing protected areas have a good coverage effect on this species. However, there are still some habitats located outside the protected areas, indicating that the current protection network has not yet achieved full coverage. These areas may face a higher risk of habitat loss and ecological disturbance.

Are Australia's Protected Areas Sufficient?

10.Growth of Protected Areas in Australia Over Time

Overall, from 1950 to 2024, new protected areas have been established every year. Among them, the number of protected areas increased the most during the periods of 1978-1979 and 2010-2011.

11.Protection Gaps Across IBRA Subregions

Note:Subregion is a more subdivided ecological unit under IBRA Bioregion. Each Subregion has similar geomorphic, climatic, soil and biome characteristics.

The coverage rate of protected areas in most ecological sub-regions is concentrated between 0% and 10%, which is significantly lower than Australia's target of protecting 30% of its land area by 2030. This indicates that there are still significant protection gaps in many ecological regions, and their ecosystems and biodiversity have not been fully guaranteed.

Conclusion

Australia has established a wide network of land protected areas, but there are significant differences in the number and scale of protected areas among states and territories. Different protected areas adopt diverse governance approaches, and the protection coverage rates of different ecological regions also vary. However, they all play significant roles in biodiversity conservation.
However, there is still an imbalance in conservation effectiveness among different ecological regions, and the important habitats of some endangered species are still located outside protected areas. Although the number of protected areas in Australia continues to increase, the coverage rate of protected areas in many IBRA Subregions is still lower than the 30% coverage requirement set by Australia's "30 by 30" target.
Therefore, future conservation efforts should not only continue to expand the network of protected areas but also pay more attention to the spatial layout and ecological representativeness of protected areas, in order to achieve more comprehensive and effective biodiversity conservation.
